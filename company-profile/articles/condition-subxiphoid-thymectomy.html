Subxiphoid Thymectomy

剑突下胸腺切除
← Back to Where China Leads

For myasthenia gravis and thymic tumours, the thymus is removed through a single 3 cm incision below the breastbone — no cutting between the ribs, no intercostal nerve injury, and both sides of the chest accessed at once. The subxiphoid approach was championed and perfected at Chinese and Asian high-volume centres, and China now runs the world's largest published experience.

What it treats & who qualifies

  • Myasthenia gravis (generalised or selected ocular forms) with or without thymoma, per neurology guidance.
  • Thymoma (Masaoka-Koga stage I–II, selected III) and larger thymic cysts or hyperplasia.
  • Patients wanting a cosmetically invisible incision (scar hidden below the breastbone).
  • Not for: clearly invasive stage III–IV thymomas needing open resection — those are planned individually.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • Chinese teams built the world's largest subxiphoid thymectomy series — from pioneering hundreds of cases to making it a standard offering at major thoracic centres.
  • A single midline incision gives symmetric access to both pleural spaces and a direct view of the aorto-pulmonary window — the hardest corner of thymectomy — without rib spreading.
  • Post-operative pain is markedly lower than lateral intercostal approaches; most patients leave hospital in 3–4 days.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Neurology and CT/MRI confirm the indication (myasthenia, thymoma, or both); acetylcholine receptor antibody status is documented.
  2. A 3 cm incision below the breastbone; a camera and instruments enter without touching intercostal nerves.
  3. Both pleural spaces are opened; the thymus and its fatty extensions are removed completely from pericardium to neck vessels, laterally to both phrenic nerves.
  4. Specimen removed in a bag; drains placed on both sides; incision closed with absorbable stitches.
  5. Most patients walk the same day; MG medication is continued unchanged and tapered later by your neurologist.

Recovery, week by week

  • Day 0–1: Same-evening walking; pain typically milder than any intercostal approach.
  • Day 2–4: Drains out; discharge; MG medications continued unchanged.
  • Week 1–2: Flight clearance around day 7; light activity resumed.
  • Month 1–6: Neurology follow-up and any MG drug taper at home, guided by your local neurologist using the Chinese operative report.

Frequently asked questions

Will removing the thymus actually improve my MG?

International studies suggest roughly half of MG patients see meaningful improvement after thymectomy combined with medical therapy; the exact rate depends on severity, age and follow-up. It is part of long-term MG management — not a guaranteed cure.

Can the surgeon see well enough through such a small incision?

Yes — the subxiphoid camera looks up at the thymus from below, giving a symmetrical view of both sides and the great vessels that intercostal approaches cannot match.

When can I resume my MG medication after surgery?

Immediately — medication is not stopped. Any taper happens months later, guided by your neurologist, and WellVoyage bridges your Chinese surgical report to your home neurology team.
