SMILE Laser Vision Correction

全飞秒激光近视手术
← Back to Where China Leads

Myopia correction without a flap: a femtosecond laser carves a lens-shaped sliver inside the cornea, which the surgeon extracts through a 2–4 mm incision. Less dry eye, more biomechanical integrity than LASIK. China is the world's largest SMILE market — over five million procedures — with certified centres in every major city and a deep bench of surgeons.

What it treats & who qualifies

  • Myopia from roughly −1.0 to −10.0 D (with or without astigmatism to −5 D), stable prescription.
  • Adequate corneal thickness measured on screening topography and pachymetry.
  • Active patients wanting a flapless option; dry-eye-prone patients.
  • Not for: unstable refraction, keratoconus-suspect topography, pregnancy, or extreme prescriptions — ICL may serve better.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • China has performed over 5 million SMILE procedures — the world's largest experience, with published complication rates below 0.1% in large series.
  • Refractive surgery in China runs at over 2 million procedures a year across hundreds of certified centres — competition and volume that keep prices at US$1,500–2,800 per eye versus US$4,000–6,000.
  • High-myopia corrections (up to -10 D) are routine — precisely the range where surgeon volume matters.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Full screening: topography, pupil measurement, dry-eye assessment, refraction stability check.
  2. The femtosecond laser creates the intracorneal lenticule — a five-minute, painless step.
  3. The surgeon extracts the lenticule through a 2–4 mm incision; no flap, nothing lifted.
  4. Vision improves within hours; most patients work the next day.
  5. Follow-up at day 1, week 1, month 1 — the day-1 check can be done at the centre, later checks at home.

Frequently asked questions

Am I too myopic for SMILE?

Corneal thickness and stability matter more than the number alone; Chinese centres routinely correct up to about -10 D where thickness allows — and will tell you honestly if an implantable lens (ICL) is the better tool for your eye.

When can I use screens again?

The next day, in sensible doses. Dryness for a few weeks is the main inconvenience — notably milder than after LASIK, which is one reason SMILE dominates in China.
