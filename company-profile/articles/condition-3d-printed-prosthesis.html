What it treats & who qualifies

  • Osteosarcoma and Ewing sarcoma around the knee, hip and shoulder after neoadjuvant chemotherapy.
  • Giant-cell tumour, chondrosarcoma and aggressive benign tumours requiring wide resection.
  • Metastatic disease with solitary bone destruction needing reconstruction.
  • Massive bone defects from infection or failed hardware where standard implants fit nothing.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • Chinese centres were among the first worldwide to approve and deploy patient-specific 3D-printed implants for limb-salvage surgery at scale, with published series running into the hundreds.
  • Custom printing means the resection margin, the joint surface and the tendon attachment points are designed around your tumour — not adapted to a catalogue.
  • Limb salvage replaces amputation in the great majority of osteosarcoma and giant-cell-tumour cases at major centres.

The procedure, step by step

  1. MRI and CT define the tumour's extent; neoadjuvant chemotherapy is given where the diagnosis calls for it.
  2. The resection is planned digitally; the prosthesis is designed to exactly fill the planned defect and printed in titanium.
  3. The tumour is removed en bloc with a wide margin; frozen sections confirm clearance.
  4. The custom prosthesis is cemented or press-fit into place; ligaments and tendons are re-attached to printed anchor points.
  5. Rehabilitation begins immediately; chemotherapy resumes on schedule.

Recovery, week by week

  • Pre-op: Neoadjuvant chemotherapy per protocol; prosthesis designed and printed during the chemo window (2–4 weeks).
  • Admission: Wide resection + implantation; ICU 1–2 days, ward 7–12 days.
  • Week 3–6: Protected weight-bearing, then progressive loading; chemotherapy cycles resume on schedule.
  • Follow-up: Quarterly imaging year 1–2, then 6-monthly; shared with your home sarcoma team.

Frequently asked questions

Will the prosthesis last or need replacement?

Printed porous-surface implants are designed for biological fixation — bone grows into them. The published Chinese series show multi-year survival; your follow-up schedule is shared with your home oncologist and orthopaedic surgeon.

Can my teenager go back to sports?

Impact sports are usually discouraged after limb salvage — but walking, cycling, swimming and often badminton are realistic. The Chinese rehab teams set expectations honestly before surgery.
