A weakened bubble on a brain artery threatens catastrophic haemorrhage. Endovascular embolization fills the aneurysm with detachable coils (or a flow-diverting stent) through a catheter threaded from the groin — no craniotomy. Chinese interventional neuroradiology teams treat enormous aneurysm volumes and publish among the world's largest series on complex and wide-necked aneurysms.

What it treats & who qualifies

  • Ruptured aneurysms — emergency treatment, ideally within 24–72 hours of the bleed.
  • Unruptured aneurysms above 7 mm, or smaller ones that are growing, symptomatic or posterior-circulation.
  • Wide-necked, giant and blood-blister aneurysms needing balloon/stent assistance or flow diversion.
  • Family history of aneurysmal bleeds with incidentally found lesions.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • Chinese centres treat one of the world's largest annual aneurysm caseloads; published Chinese series on wide-necked, blood-blister-like and giant aneurysms are reference literature in the field.
  • Dual antiplatelet management, balloon-assisted and stent-assisted techniques, and flow diverters (including domestic devices like Tubridge) are routine tools.
  • Cost is typically US$12,000–25,000 all-in versus US$75,000–150,000+ self-pay in the US.

The procedure, step by step

  1. DSA angiography (or high-quality CTA) characterises the aneurysm: neck width, dome, vessel relationship.
  2. Under general anaesthesia, a catheter is navigated from the groin to the cerebral circulation.
  3. Coils are deployed into the aneurysm sac — assisted by balloon or stent where the neck is wide.
  4. For uncoilable anatomy, a flow-diverting stent is placed across the neck; the aneurysm thromboses over weeks.
  5. Neurological observation overnight; uncomplicated cases are discharged at 3–5 days.

Recovery, week by week

  • Day 0: Procedure 1–3 hours; overnight neuro-observation.
  • Day 1–3: Walking, normal diet; ruptured cases stay longer for vasospasm management (up to 2 weeks).
  • Day 3–5: Discharge for unruptured cases; antiplatelet plan written for home.
  • Follow-up: Angiography or non-invasive vessel imaging at 6–12 months — arranged in your home city with the Chinese records.

Frequently asked questions

Coiling or clipping — which is right for me?

Morphology decides: wide-necked, complex-shaped aneurysms may need stent assistance or diversion; certain configurations (e.g., middle-cerebral bifurcation) are sometimes better clipped surgically. High-volume Chinese centres run both teams and will tell you which is genuinely better for your anatomy — WellVoyage collects both opinions.

Do I need antiplatelet drugs forever?

Only with stent-assisted techniques — usually a few months to a year. Bare-coil cases need nothing long-term. Your discharge plan states the exact regimen for your home physician.
