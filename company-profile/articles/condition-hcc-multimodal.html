What it treats & who qualifies

  • Barcelona stages 0–B: very-early and early HCC (resection, ablation), intermediate (TACE-based).
  • Advanced HCC where systemic therapy — immunotherapy combinations — is the backbone.
  • Hepatitis-B or C carriers needing integrated antiviral-plus-oncology management.
  • Large/borderline resectable tumours considered for conversion therapy.
  • Not for: decompensated cirrhosis with poor liver reserve — transplant evaluation is the honest answer.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • China accounts for roughly half of global HCC cases and has produced much of the world's guidance on resection, ablation and combination immunotherapy for advanced disease.
  • Published Chinese series report resection and ablation results for small HCC (5-year survival above 60–70%) and Chinese-led trials defined the modern immunotherapy-plus-anti-VEGF standard for advanced HCC.
  • All modalities under one roof: surgery, interventional radiology, radiotherapy and systemic therapy conference together weekly.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Multiphase CT/MRI with AFP establishes diagnosis (often without biopsy, per guidelines); liver function is graded (Child-Pugh).
  2. Early-stage disease goes to resection or ablation (radiofrequency/microwave) — choice depends on size, number, location and liver reserve.
  3. Intermediate disease goes to TACE (transarterial chemoembolisation); combinations with ablation are standard.
  4. Advanced disease receives immunotherapy plus anti-VEGF therapy — the combination largely defined by Chinese-led pivotal trials.
  5. The same MDT re-reviews every 6–8 weeks and re-routes patients across modalities as disease responds or progresses.

Recovery, week by week

  • Work-up: 1 week: imaging review, Child-Pugh scoring, HBV viral load, MDT conference.
  • Resection: 7–12 day admission; ICG planning on the table; histology sets the adjuvant plan.
  • TACE / ablation: 2–4 day admissions per session, repeatable per schedule.
  • Systemic: Outpatient cycles every 3 weeks — often partially continued at home with the written protocol.

Frequently asked questions

I have hepatitis B — does that change my treatment in China?

It makes antiviral control part of the cancer treatment, and Chinese centres manage HBV-related HCC every day — antiviral suppression before, during and after therapy is standard there in a way smaller programmes often overlook.

Can I do some treatment in China and continue at home?
