ERCP — Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography

ERCP 胆胰内镜诊疗
← Back to Where China Leads

Bile-duct stones, strictures and jaundice-defining blockages treated through a scope passed from the mouth — no cutting at all in most cases. Chinese GI centres run some of the highest-volume ERCP services in the world; their endoscopists handle complex intrahepatic stones and post-operative strictures that low-volume units refer out.

What it treats & who qualifies

  • Choledocholithiasis (bile-duct stones) including difficult intrahepatic and impacted stones.
  • Benign strictures (post-surgical, chronic pancreatitis-related) needing dilation or stenting.
  • Malignant biliary obstruction requiring drainage (metal or plastic stenting).
  • Ampullary lesions; selected bile-duct leaks after surgery.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • Chinese GI centres publish some of the world's largest ERCP series — complex stones, intrahepatic disease, benign strictures — with published success rates above 90% for difficult stones.
  • Spyglass cholangioscopy, EUS-guided access, and covered metal stenting are standard inventory at major centres.
  • Cost: roughly US$4,000–8,000 per therapeutic ERCP versus US$15,000–30,000 self-pay in the US.

The procedure, step by step

  1. MRCP (a non-invasive scan) maps the ducts first; ERCP is reserved for therapy, not just diagnosis.
  2. Under sedation, a duodenoscope is passed to the duodenum and the papilla of Vater is cannulated.
  3. Contrast X-ray defines the anatomy; stones are extracted with baskets/balloons, strictures dilated and stented.
  4. Cholangioscopy (Spyglass) visualises the duct lining directly for large/impacted stones and tumour assessment.
  5. Most therapeutic cases are day-case or one-night admissions; repeat procedures are scheduled as needed.

Recovery, week by week

  • Day 0: Procedure 30–90 minutes; sedation, not general anaesthesia.
  • Hours 0–6: Bed rest, pancreatitis-watch protocol (bloods at 4–6 hours).
  • Day 1: Discharge for uncomplicated cases; stent calendar issued.
  • Follow-up: Stricture patients get an exchange calendar; stone patients a 6–12 month ultrasound at home.

Frequently asked questions

Is ERCP safe?

Pancreatitis is the main risk (a few percent, usually mild); volume is the strongest safety factor known, and Chinese high-volume endoscopists run some of the largest published cohorts with correspondingly tuned complication management.

I have a stent — how do exchanges work if I live far away?

Plastic stents are exchanged every ~3 months, metal stents last longer. Your exchange calendar is written down; exchanges can happen at home if a local ERCP centre exists, or be bundled into a return visit.
