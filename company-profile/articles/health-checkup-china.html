What every WellVoyage checkup includes

  • Pre-travel review — we read your history and symptoms, then recommend the package that fits rather than the most expensive one.
  • Appointment booked in your name — hospital international department, no queueing on arrival.
  • Interpreter accompaniment — English-speaking coordinator with you for every module, including the consultations.

  • Onward referral if needed — if screening finds something that needs treatment, we map the specialist pathway, in China or at home.

Why do a checkup in China

  • Imaging you can actually book — Low-dose chest CT, 3T MRI and PET-CT are routine appointments at top-tier Chinese hospitals, not rationed referrals. Screening guests routinely add whole-body imaging that would take months to authorise elsewhere.
  • Same week, not same quarter — Appointments open within days. A full screening programme is typically completed in one to three mornings, with most laboratory and imaging results reported the same day.
  • Endoscopy without the referral fight — Gastroscopy and colonoscopy under light sedation are standard self-pay services. No waiting list, no gatekeeping referral, and the operator will biopsy on the spot if something needs it.
  • An English report a physician walks you through — You leave with a written English report and a scheduled walk-through with a physician, so the numbers mean something before you get on the plane.

Frequently asked questions

What does a checkup in China cost?

We quote on request. Chinese hospitals set self-pay rates individually depending on the modules selected and the hospital tier, so a published figure would be misleading for most guests. Send us the package you are interested in and we return a written, itemised quote within 24 hours — hospital fees, English report, interpreter and transfers all listed separately, before you book anything.

Do I need a referral or a diagnosis to book a checkup in China?

No. Screening is a self-pay service at the international departments we work with, so no referral letter is required. We do ask for your history so that the physician can interpret results against context.

How many days should I plan for?

Most guests set aside two to three days. A one-day executive programme is possible but leaves less room for add-on imaging or a sedated endoscopy, which need their own slot.

Is the report in English, and can my own doctor use it?

Yes. You receive a written English report with the underlying images and laboratory values, which your physician at home can read and act on.

What happens if the screening finds something?

You get a physician walk-through and a named next step. If treatment is advised, we map the specialist pathway — in China or in your home country — with cost and timeline, so the decision stays yours.

Can screening be combined with a treatment trip?

Yes. Family members frequently screen while a patient is being treated, and we schedule both around the same travel window.
