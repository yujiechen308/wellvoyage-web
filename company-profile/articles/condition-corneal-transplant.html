Corneal Transplantation (PK / DMEK / DALK)

角膜移植
← Back to Where China Leads

A clouded or damaged cornea — from infection, dystrophy, injury or keratoconus — is replaced with donor tissue. Chinese eye centres built one of the world's largest corneal transplant programmes on the back of an organised eye-bank network covering every province, which is why the waiting time for donor tissue is short where other countries queue for years.

What it treats & who qualifies

  • Corneal scarring from infection (herpes, bacterial keratitis), trauma or burns.
  • Keratoconus and other ectasias unsuitable for lens or cross-linking.
  • Endothelial disease: Fuchs' dystrophy, pseudophakic bullous keratopathy.
  • Corneal dystrophies and congenital opacities; failed previous grafts needing regrafting.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • China performs over 10,000 corneal transplants a year across 60+ networked eye banks covering all 31 provinces — among the largest programmes in the world.
  • Published Chinese series report 5-year graft clarity above 75% for penetrating keratoplasty, with DALK and DMEK techniques standardised at major centres.
  • Waiting time for matched donor tissue is typically weeks — in many Western public systems it is years.

Treatment options, compared

Deep anterior lamellar keratoplasty (DALK)Keratoconus, stromal disease with healthy endotheliumKeeps your own endothelium — rejection risk dramatically lower.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Work-up: corneal topography, endothelial cell count, cause-of-opacity assessment.
  2. Donor tissue is matched and prepared; the technique (full-thickness PK, partial DALK, or endothelial DMEK) is chosen by which corneal layer is diseased.
  3. The diseased cornea is removed or dissected; donor tissue is sutured or positioned.
  4. Day-case or one-night admission; steroid drops follow a months-long taper.
  5. Sutures out and refraction stabilised over 6–18 months; glasses or CLAPT lenses finalise vision.

Recovery, week by week

  • Day 0: Procedure 40–90 minutes; day-case or one night.
  • Week 1: Drop regimen begins; follow-up at day 1 and week 1 before flying.
  • Months 1–6: Selective suture removal (PK); refraction stabilises gradually.
  • Year 1+: Annual checks; graft survival is the metric to watch — 75%+ clarity at 5 years in published Chinese series.

Which technique will I get?
