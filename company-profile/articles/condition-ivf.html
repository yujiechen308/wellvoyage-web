Eggs retrieved, fertilised in the laboratory, embryos grown to blastocyst and transferred — the fundamentals are universal, but success rates, waiting lists and costs are not. China performs over one million assisted-reproduction cycles a year, the largest national programme on earth, with licensed centres publishing live-birth rates for under-35s around 45–50% per fresh transfer.

What it treats & who qualifies

  • Infertility: tubal disease, endometriosis, ovulatory dysfunction, male-factor, unexplained.
  • Advanced reproductive age with diminished ovarian reserve needing efficient, well-monitored cycles.
  • Fertility preservation ahead of medical therapy (cancer, autoimmune treatment).
  • Recurrent implantation failure or miscarriage needing a diagnostic deep-dive plus a strong embryology lab.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • China runs over one million ART cycles a year — the world's largest programme (CSRM data), with roughly 600 licensed centres.
  • Published Chinese registry data show clinical pregnancy rates of 50–60% and live-birth rates near 45–50% for under-35 women per fresh transfer.
  • A self-pay cycle costs US$4,000–8,000 versus US$15,000–25,000+ in the US — and there is no queue to start.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Ovarian reserve testing and semen analysis; a stimulation protocol is selected for your profile.
  2. Ovarian stimulation runs ~10–12 days with ultrasound and hormone monitoring; trigger injection matures the eggs.
  3. Eggs are retrieved under light sedation (a 20-minute procedure); fertilisation is by standard IVF or ICSI.
  4. Embryos culture to day 3 or blastocyst; one is transferred, the rest vitrified for later.
  5. A pregnancy test at two weeks; frozen-embryo transfers follow in natural or medicated cycles as needed.

Recovery, week by week

  • Day 1–10: Stimulation with ultrasound monitoring every 2–3 days.
  • Trigger day: Egg collection ~36 hours after trigger; 20-minute procedure, sedation.
  • Day +1 to +5: Fertilisation and blastocyst culture updates by message.
  • Transfer: Fresh (day 5) or frozen (planned cycle); two-week wait; then follow-up wherever you live.

Frequently asked questions

How long do we need to stay in China?

A fresh cycle is about 3–5 weeks door to door; the Chinese team can also structure your protocol so monitoring happens at home and you fly in only for retrieval and transfer (about 10 days). WellVoyage builds the timetable with the centre before you commit.

Are the clinics licensed?

Yes — assisted reproduction in China is regulated by the NHC with a strict licensing system (~600 approved centres). WellVoyage works only with licensed units and can provide the licence details of your centre in writing.

What happens to our frozen embryos if we go home?

They stay vitrified at the centre until you return for a sibling transfer — or you can arrange supervised transport through a certified courier service. Chinese centres handle cross-border embryo logistics routinely.
