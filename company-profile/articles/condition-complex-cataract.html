A routine cataract operation is fifteen minutes; a complex one — with a traumatised eye, a weak or missing lens support, a pupil that will not dilate, or a prior vitrectomy — is where surgical volume decides outcomes. China performs over four million cataract operations a year, the largest volume on earth, and its centres have seen every anatomical variant of difficult lens there is.

What it treats & who qualifies

  • Mature or hypermature ('white') cataracts; rock-hard lenses.
  • Subluxated or dislocated lenses (Marfan, homocystinuria, trauma).
  • Pseudoexfoliation with unstable zonules; uveitic cataracts.
  • Post-vitrectomy and silicone-oil eyes; previous ocular trauma with torn capsules.
  • Patients wanting premium lenses (multifocal, toric, EDOF) in anatomically normal eyes.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • China performs over 4 million cataract operations a year — the world's largest volume — and publishes large series on subluxated lenses, traumatic cataracts, pseudoexfoliation and post-vitrectomy eyes.
  • Premium intraocular lens options (multifocal, toric, EDOF) are widely available at US$1,500–4,000 per eye all-in.
  • Complex cases (capsular tension rings, iris hooks, glued/scleral-fixated IOLs) are routine inventory at high-volume centres.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Biometry and corneal topography size the eye and select the intraocular lens power.
  2. Phacoemulsification removes the clouded lens through a 2–3 mm incision.
  3. For complex anatomy, adjunct devices (capsular tension ring, iris hooks) stabilise the capsular bag.
  4. The IOL is implanted; in cases without capsular support, a scleral-fixated lens is sutured in place.
  5. Same-day discharge; drops for four weeks; refraction checked at one month.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a multifocal lens in one eye and monofocal in the other?

Mixed strategies are available and often sensible; the Chinese team models your visual expectations with trial lenses before surgery — including honest discussion of halos at night.

I've had vitrectomy before — does that complicate things?

It does, and post-vitrectomy cataracts are a daily case mix at Chinese retina-cataract centres. The key is the team handles both the lens and the silicon-oil/gas history in one plan.
