Eggs retrieved after ovarian stimulation and vitrified — flash-frozen in glass-like state — holding your fertility at today's age for years. Chinese reproductive centres have some of the world's largest vitrification experience, because the technique was scaled in China's enormous IVF programmes.

What it treats & who qualifies

  • Women wishing to preserve fertility ahead of gonadotoxic therapy (chemo, radiation, immunosuppression).
  • Elective social freezing where regulations and centre policy permit (medical indications are fully supported).
  • Endometriosis surgery or ovarian surgery planned (tissue loss risk).
  • Best results under 35; honest counselling offered above 38.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • Chinese centres publish large vitrification cohorts with post-warm survival rates above 90% and healthy live births from eggs frozen 5–10+ years — the technique's Chinese scale is second only to its IVF scale.
  • A stimulation-plus-retrieval cycle costs US$3,000–5,000 in China versus US$10,000–20,000 in the US, making multiple-cycle banking realistic.
  • Medical indications (approaching chemo/radiotherapy, surgery, endometriosis) are standard and fully supported with fast-track pathways.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Ovarian reserve testing (AMH, antral follicle count) sizes the stimulation protocol.
  2. Hormonal stimulation runs ~10–12 days with frequent monitoring; a trigger matures the eggs.
  3. Eggs are retrieved under light sedation — a 20-minute procedure; ~10–20 eggs is a good typical yield.
  4. Mature eggs are vitrified the same day and stored in the centre's cryobank.
  5. When you return — months or years later — eggs are warmed, fertilised, and transferred as an IVF cycle.

Recovery, week by week

  • Cycle: 10–12 days of injections with monitoring; retrieval day 12-ish.
  • Retrieval day: 20-minute procedure; rest half a day; fly home 1–2 days later.
  • Storage: Annual fee; chain-of-custody documentation; status reports on request.
  • Later use: Warm, fertilise, transfer — as an IVF cycle at the same centre or coordinated from home.

Frequently asked questions

How long can eggs stay frozen?

Indefinitely for practical purposes — vitrified eggs show no deterioration in published cohorts over 5–10+ years of storage. Chinese cryobanks store under continuous monitoring with documented chain-of-custody.

How many eggs is enough?

General guidance: ~15–20 frozen eggs gives a strong chance of one live birth at a younger freeze-age. The Chinese centre's own age-specific data informs your plan — WellVoyage shares it in writing before your cycle.

Can unmarried women freeze eggs in China?

Chinese national rules currently restrict elective egg freezing for unmarried women without a medical indication; medical indications (e.g., before gonadotoxic therapy) are fully supported. WellVoyage confirms your eligibility status with the centre before any planning.
