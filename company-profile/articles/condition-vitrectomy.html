What it treats & who qualifies

  • Rhegmatogenous and complex retinal detachment; giant tears.
  • Proliferative diabetic retinopathy with traction detachment or non-clearing haemorrhage.
  • Macular holes, epiretinal membranes; dislocated lens fragments.
  • Retinal complications of high myopia (myopic foveoschisis, myopic macular holes).

Why patients travel to China for this

  • China performs over 300,000 vitrectomies a year (Chinese Ophthalmological Society retina section data) — driven by the world's largest diabetic and high-myopia populations.
  • Published Chinese series report anatomical success for rhegmatogenous detachment above 90% and meaningful visual improvement in over 80% of proliferative diabetic retinopathy cases.
  • Combined anti-VEGF injection plus surgery pathways are fully integrated — the drug, the surgery and the laser in one centre.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Detailed fundus examination and OCT/ultrasound mapping of the pathology.
  2. Micro-incisional (25G/27G) vitrectomy removes the vitreous gel and any blood.
  3. Membranes are peeled; the retina is flattened; tears are sealed with laser or cryo.
  4. The eye is filled with gas or silicone oil as a temporary scaffold.
  5. Positioning instructions follow; oil removal is a second short procedure when needed.

Recovery, week by week

  • Day 0: Surgery 45–120 minutes; positioning instructions depend on tamponade.
  • Days 1–7: Positioning (if gas); drops; posture discipline is the patient's main job.
  • Weeks 2–6: Gas absorbs; NO FLYING until cleared — the centre times surgery around your travel window.
  • Months 1–6: Silicone-oil removal when used; anti-VEGF injections per protocol, transferable to home retina specialists.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't I fly with gas in my eye?

Altitude makes intraocular gas expand and can crush the retina — a hard physiological fact, not a formality. Chinese centres time your surgery so the gas absorbs before your flight, or use oil tamponade if you must fly sooner.

One operation or several?

Detachment repair is usually one; diabetic disease often needs staged surgery plus ongoing injections. The Chinese team maps the whole sequence — including which parts your home retina specialist can take over — before you commit.
