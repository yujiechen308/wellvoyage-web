Heart Transplantation

心脏移植
← Back to Where China Leads

For end-stage heart failure, a transplant replaces a failing heart with a donor organ. China's transplant centres concentrated in a small number of licensed, high-volume institutions perform large annual volumes with published 1-year survival consistently above 90% — among the best in the world — and at costs dramatically below Western self-pay rates.

What it treats & who qualifies

  • End-stage heart failure refractory to maximal therapy (ischaemic, dilated, peripartum, congenital).
  • Intractable arrhythmias or refractory angina unsuitable for revascularisation.
  • Some congenital heart disease variants failing in adulthood (Fontan circulation, etc.).
  • Psychosocial suitability and a committed follow-up structure at home — the Chinese team verifies both.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • Major Chinese transplant centres publish 1-year survival above 90% and 5-year survival above 80% — matching or exceeding ISHLT international benchmarks — driven by concentrated volume at licensed centres.
  • Waiting time for a matched organ at China's major centres is often shorter than at home because donor allocation within China's COTRS system is national and centrally coordinated.
  • Self-pay all-in cost of roughly US$90,000–140,000 versus US$1.4M+ billed charges in the US.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Extensive work-up: blood type, HLA and antibody screening, cancer clearance, pulmonary vascular testing, psychosocial evaluation.
  2. Listing with the national organ allocation system (COTRS); patients usually stay near the centre while waiting.
  3. When a matched organ is allocated, the recipient is admitted; the donor heart is transported on ice or perfusion machine.
  4. The failed heart is excised and the donor heart is anastomosed (biatrial or bicaval technique); the heart is re-perfused and resumes beating.
  5. ICU recovery, immunosuppression induction, then staged rehabilitation and lifelong immunosuppression management.

Recovery, week by week

  • Week 0–2: Work-up and listing; patients stay near the centre with family support arranged.
  • Admission: When a matched organ is allocated, surgery happens within hours; ICU 5–10 days.
  • Month 1: Ward rehabilitation; induction immunosuppression; biopsy surveillance begins.
  • Month 2–3: Discharge and rehabilitation; fly home once stable; home team takes over with the full English file.

Frequently asked questions

Who licenses and oversees organ donation in China?

Donation and allocation run through China's national system (COTRS), with voluntary non-directed donation after death. Transplants for international patients are performed only at officially licensed centres; WellVoyage works exclusively within that licensed framework and documents the full chain.

Can my home centre manage my post-transplant care?

Yes, and that is the plan from day one: Chinese centres prepare a full English discharge pack — operative notes, immunosuppression levels, biopsy schedule — that your home transplant or heart-failure team continues against.
