IMRT for Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma is rare in the West and common in southern China — and the radiotherapy that cures it (intensity-modulated radiation therapy, sculpting dose around the brainstem, salivary glands and optic nerves) was perfected where the patients are. Chinese centres publish 5-year overall survival above 80% with long-term toxicity figures Western series rarely see, simply because they treat NPC every single day.

What it treats & who qualifies

  • All curable stages of nasopharyngeal carcinoma — IMRT is the backbone of every stage.
  • Stage II–III adding concurrent chemotherapy; selected advanced stages with induction or adjuvant strategies.
  • Residual or recurrent local disease considered for re-irradiation or surgery (nasopharyngectomy) in expert centres.
  • EBV-DNA surveillance integrated into follow-up.

Why patients travel to China for this

  • China sees a large share of the world's NPC cases; leading Guangzhou centres publish 5-year overall survival above 80% and local control above 92% with IMRT (Radiotherapy and Oncology, 2020).
  • Intensity-modulated and image-guided techniques spare the parotid glands — patients keep saliva, taste and quality of life decades later.
  • Chinese-led trials defined the modern concurrent chemoradiotherapy and adjuvant immunotherapy standards for advanced NPC.

The procedure, step by step

  1. MRI defines the tumour extent; Epstein-Barr virus DNA levels guide staging and response monitoring.
  2. A custom immobilisation mask is made; the IMRT plan sculpts dose around brainstem, cord, parotids and optic structures.
  3. Daily image guidance ensures millimetre accuracy; treatment runs over ~33 weekday sessions.
  4. Concurrent weekly cisplatin is standard for advanced stages; supportive care manages mucositis.
  5. Post-treatment endoscopy and MRI confirm response; EBV-DNA surveillance continues for years.

Recovery, week by week

  • Week 0: Mask-making, planning, plan review; treatment calendar handed over.
  • Weeks 1–7: Daily weekday sessions (~15 minutes); acute mucositis managed with the centre's supportive protocol.
  • Weeks 8–10: Recovery from mucositis; taste and saliva gradually return over months.
  • Follow-up: Endoscopy + MRI + EBV-DNA at 3-month intervals year 1 — images shared with your home ENT.

Frequently asked questions

Why travel when there is a radiotherapy machine at home?

The machine matters less than the plan and the practice: NPC is a large fraction of a Guangzhou centre's daily work versus a handful of cases yearly elsewhere — and dosimetry for this tumour is unforgiving of inexperience.

Six weeks is a long time away from home — can it be shorter?

Radiotherapy fractions cannot be safely compressed for NPC. WellVoyage arranges long-stay housing near the centre; many patients treat the stay as a working remote arrangement or bring family.
